# § 3.7 Privacy concern cases

(a) *Privacy concern cases.* The employer shall not enter the employee's name on the log for a privacy concern case. Instead, the employer shall enter "privacy case" in the space normally used for the employee's name. A privacy concern case is one that involves:

(1) An injury or illness to an intimate body part or the reproductive system;

(2) An injury or illness resulting from a sexual assault;

(3) A mental illness;

(4) HIV infection, hepatitis, or tuberculosis;

(5) A needlestick injury or a cut from a sharp object that is contaminated with another person's blood or other potentially infectious material; or

(6) Any other illness, if the employee voluntarily requests that the name not be entered on the log.

(b) *Confidential list.* The employer shall keep a separate, confidential list of the case numbers and employee names for the privacy concern cases, so that the cases can be updated and the information can be provided to the Administration on request.

(c) *Descriptive information.* If the employer has a reasonable basis to believe that information describing a privacy concern case may be personally identifiable even without the employee's name, the employer may use discretion in describing the injury or illness on the log and on the incident report kept under [§ 3.3](CFR:99-3.3), while entering enough information to identify the cause of the incident and the general severity of the injury or illness.

(d) *Disclosure.* Except as required by this section or by [§ 3.6](CFR:99-3.6), the employer shall not disclose the confidential list or the names on it to anyone other than the Administration or a State agency operating an approved plan.
