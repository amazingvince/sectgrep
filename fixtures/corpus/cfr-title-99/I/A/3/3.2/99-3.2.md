# § 3.2 Recording criteria

(a) *Basic requirement.* Each employer required by this part to keep records shall record each fatality, injury, and illness that is work-related, is a new case, and meets one or more of the general recording criteria in paragraph (d) of this section.

(b) *Work-relatedness.* An injury or illness is work-related if an event or exposure in the work environment either caused or contributed to the resulting condition, or significantly aggravated a pre-existing injury or illness. Work-relatedness is presumed for injuries and illnesses resulting from events or exposures in the work environment, unless an exception in paragraph (c) of this section applies.

(c) *Exceptions.* An injury or illness is not work-related if:

(1) At the time of the injury or illness, the employee was present in the work environment as a member of the general public rather than as an employee;

(2) The injury or illness involves signs or symptoms that surface at work but result solely from a non-work-related event or exposure that occurs outside the work environment;

(3) The injury or illness results solely from voluntary participation in a wellness program or in a medical, fitness, or recreational activity;

(4) The injury or illness is solely the result of an employee eating, drinking, or preparing food or drink for personal consumption; or

(5) The illness is the common cold or flu.

(d) *General recording criteria.* An injury or illness is recordable if it results in any of the following: death; days away from work; restricted work or transfer to another job; medical treatment beyond first aid, as those terms are defined in [§ 3.9](CFR:99-3.9); loss of consciousness; or a significant injury or illness diagnosed by a physician or other licensed health care professional.

(e) *New case.* An injury or illness is a new case if the employee has not previously experienced a recorded injury or illness of the same type that affects the same part of the body, or if the employee previously experienced such a case but had recovered completely and an event or exposure in the work environment caused the signs or symptoms to reappear.

(f) *Hazardous conditions.* A hazardous condition, as defined in [§ 1.2](CFR:99-1.2#hazardous-condition), that is discovered but has not caused an injury or illness is not entered on the log; it is recorded with its interim measures as required by [§ 1.4(c)](CFR:99-1.4#c).
