# § 2.10 Exit routes; design and construction

(a) *Number of exit routes.* At least two exit routes, as defined in [§ 2.2](CFR:99-2.2#exit-route), shall be available in a workplace to permit prompt evacuation of employees and other occupants during an emergency. The exit routes shall be located as far away as practicable from each other so that if one exit route is blocked by fire or smoke, employees can evacuate using the second. More than two exit routes shall be provided where the number of employees, the size of the building, its occupancy, or the arrangement of the workplace is such that all employees would not be able to evacuate safely through two. A single exit route is permitted where the number of employees, the size of the building, its occupancy, or the arrangement of the workplace is such that all employees would be able to evacuate safely through one.

(b) *Width and height.* The ceiling of an exit route shall be at least 7 feet 6 inches (2.3 m) high, with any projection from the ceiling at least 6 feet 8 inches (2.0 m) above the floor. An exit access shall be at least 28 inches (71 cm) wide at all points. Where there is only one exit access leading to an exit or exit discharge, the width of the exit and exit discharge shall be at least equal to the width of the exit access. Objects that project into the exit route shall not reduce the width of the exit route to less than the minimum width required.

(c) *Construction.* An exit shall be separated from other parts of the workplace by fire-resistant materials rated at one hour where the exit connects three or fewer stories, and at two hours where the exit connects four or more stories. An exit shall open only to the exit discharge or to another exit. Openings into an exit shall be protected by a self-closing fire door that remains closed or automatically closes in an emergency.

(d) *Exit discharge.* Each exit discharge, as defined in [§ 2.2](CFR:99-2.2#exit-discharge), shall lead directly outside or to a street, walkway, refuge area, public way, or open space with access to the outside, and shall be large enough to accommodate the building occupants likely to use the exit route.

(e) *Doors and locks.* An exit door shall be unlocked from the inside, shall be free of any device or alarm that could restrict emergency use if the device or alarm fails, and shall be a side-hinged door that swings out in the direction of exit travel if the room is designed to be occupied by more than 50 people or contains high-hazard contents.

(f) *Capacity.* Exit routes shall support the maximum permitted occupant load for each floor served, and the capacity of an exit route shall not decrease in the direction of exit route travel to the exit discharge.
