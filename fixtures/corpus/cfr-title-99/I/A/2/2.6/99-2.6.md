# § 2.6 Portable ladders

(a) *Application.* This section applies to each portable ladder, as defined in [§ 2.2](CFR:99-2.2#portable-ladder), in addition to the requirements of [§ 2.5](CFR:99-2.5).

(b) *Duty rating.* Each portable ladder shall be marked with its duty rating and shall not be loaded beyond the maximum load for that rating in the following table.

| Duty rating | Type | Maximum load (person plus materials) |
|---|---|---|
| Special duty | IAA | 375 pounds (170 kg) |
| Extra heavy duty | IA | 300 pounds (136 kg) |
| Heavy duty | I | 250 pounds (113 kg) |
| Medium duty | II | 225 pounds (102 kg) |
| Light duty | III | 200 pounds (91 kg) |

(c) *Setup.* A non-self-supporting portable ladder shall be used at an angle such that the horizontal distance from the top support to the foot of the ladder is approximately one-quarter of the working length of the ladder. A self-supporting ladder shall be used only with its spreaders locked in the fully open position.

(d) *Extension above landing.* When a portable ladder is used to gain access to an upper landing surface, the side rails shall extend at least 3 feet (0.9 m) above the landing surface, or the ladder shall be secured at its top to a rigid support and a grab device shall be provided.

(e) *Securing and placement.* A portable ladder shall be secured, or held by another employee, when it is used on a slippery surface or where it may be displaced by workplace activity or traffic. A portable ladder shall not be placed on boxes, barrels, or other unstable bases to obtain additional height, and shall not be moved, shifted, or extended while an employee is on it.

(f) *Top step and cap.* An employee shall not stand on the top step or cap of a stepladder, or on the top three rungs of a straight or extension ladder, unless the ladder is designed for that use.
